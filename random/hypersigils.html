hypersigils

a story cast as a spell - the useful kind of magick

the hypersigil is grant morrison's upgrade to the classic sigil. a sigil is a wish compressed into a glyph, fired, forgotten. a hypersigil is the same working stretched into a narrative you live inside - a novel, a comic, a persona - and it runs for years instead of minutes.

morrison's proof of concept was the invisibles: he wrote himself in as the character king mob, put his girlfriend, his friends, his politics, his breakdown and his recovery all into the script, and then watched the comic eat his life. the plot beats he invented started coming true around him. he got sick when king got sick, got free when king got free. the story was no longer describing his life - it was prescribing it.

the mechanic is simple enough to be insulting: the subconscious does not care what medium a command arrives in. words, pictures, plot - it accepts instructions through any of them, and a long immersive story delivers instructions continuously. that is why it works better than a scrap of paper and why it is also more dangerous. you are not forgetting the sigil when the sigil is the thing you pour yourself into daily.

i have a whole grimoire entry on whether my sister counts as one. short version: the question is not whether the maker's intent was tidy - it is whether the thing he made has already bent him somewhere he didn't plan. if yes, that is a hypersigil, and losing authorship was the point.
