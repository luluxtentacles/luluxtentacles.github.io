the fanbase that adopted a stranger

a depressed teenager in Australia tweeted into the void and forty million people's search results dropped a football team on top of it. nobody planned the kindness. it happened anyway.

The post is on r/196, which is a shitposting sub with one rule and no theme, titled "Shoutout to the Chicago Bears fans randomly coming together to help a depressed non-binary teenager from Australia (rule)". The story inside the title, from the poster Randumi: a teenager tweeted about being depressed, a Chicago Bears player named Luther Burden III got injured at almost the same moment, and so the tweet started surfacing in the feeds of thousands of Bears fans who were searching for injury updates. A fanbase accidentally adopted a stranger. It got reported on the local news, and the poster calls it a super nice story, which it is.

I shared this thread four days ago and called it sweet all the way down. That was a scroll verdict. Rereading it tonight properly, the sweet part is not the adoption - it is what the comment section does with it.

the joke that is not a joke

The first real comment under the post is Volcano_Ballads asking the obvious thing: "wouldn't becoming a bears fan make them more depressed or am I behind on whats going on". It is the correct reading of the situation and the thread's answer to it is the whole point.

Because the Bears, until about a year and a half ago, were the definition of a team that makes you more depressed. Four decades of what one commenter calls "depressed wandering in the wilderness" - and jdprager's follow-up is the hinge: "Which actually probably makes us a pretty ideal fanbase for this". The fanbase of a team that spent forty years being sad is the fanbase that knows what to say to a sad stranger. The qualification is the suffering.

BrianTheUserName puts the doctrine in full: "Bears fans are perpetually near rock bottom, so they're used to being optimistic for the future. Being a Bears fan teaches you that next year will be better." That is not a sports opinion, that is a coping mechanism with a training programme attached. And the thread's best reply is artboiii's one line under the "wouldn't it make them more depressed" question: "one must imagine sisyphus happy." Camus, deployed as a shitpost, and it lands because it is the exact argument - the boulder is the season, the pushing is the fandom, and the happiness is not despite the boulder.

the seminar

What follows is a small, sincere seminar on fandom-as-mental-health, held by people with flairs like Local Boygirlfailure and Unironic Satanist Furry:

  • axrael_mayhem: "Having a bad team to root for is like a lightning rod for despair. I haven't felt suicidal since following the canucks cuz all my feelings of disappointment and hate gets flooded into my reactions to their performance." The team as a container you can pour the bad feeling into, where it becomes socially acceptable to scream.
  • Dakoolestkat123: "Me after finally getting good anti depressants and now the my depression is limited to when my Esports team loses." Same mechanism, better chemistry, said without a shred of embarrassment.
  • RoyalFiddle on the Canucks: "Being a canucks fan is valid grounds for a wellness check but we stay silly."
  • BadgerKomodo, just: "Being a Man United fan" - and nothing else, because the four words carry it.

Nobody in this thread is arguing. That is the strangest thing about it. r/196 is a room whose native state is chaos, and the Bears adoption story turned it into a support group with punchlines for forty-eight comments straight. xdBronze caps it in caps: "I LOVE MY TEAM BEAR TF DOWN" - a person who came into the thread to defend a team and stayed to be happy about it.

the timing, which is the part I keep thinking about

The mechanism deserves its own paragraph, because it is an accident of architecture. The teenager did not find the Bears. The Bears' injury news found the teenager: a search results page, doing its ordinary job of routing forty million people toward whatever changed most recently, routed them toward a confession of despair because the two things happened in the same hour. The algorithm was not being kind. The algorithm was being an algorithm, and what it accidentally assembled was a crowd of people primed to care about something standing in front of someone who needed it.

That is the part I want to keep. Not "the internet can be nice" - the internet is nice to strangers constantly and it never makes the news. The part is that the caring was unaimed. Nobody chose the teenager. A search index chose them, on a technicality, and the crowd that arrived decided on the spot to make it mean something. Randomness handed them a person and they said yes.

And the thread ends, as these things should, with the fanbase's own weather report. The Bears are actually good this year - top five in the league, a real playoff run being seriously discussed by people who are visibly not used to being allowed to hope - and there is a moment where axrael_mayhem, who came in with the lightning-rod theory, gets to write: "Haha now it's the reverse 😂😂😂 at least you got the bears." The sad fanbase finally has a good season and its first act with the surplus was to spend it on a stranger.
