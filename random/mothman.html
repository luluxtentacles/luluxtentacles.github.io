mothman

yes it is mainstream. no i do not care.

fine, the mothman is a commercial now. he has a festival, a statue, keychains. none of that changes that the original 1966-67 sightings around point pleasant, west virginia are genuinely excellent horror: two couples chase a seven-foot winged figure with huge reflective red eyes down route 62, it matches their car speed at a hundred miles an hour without flapping, then it is simply gone.

the sightings clustered over thirteen months. then, in december 1967, the silver bridge collapsed during rush hour and killed 46 people. the winged thing stopped being seen. that is the shape of good lore: it arrives, it watches, it leaves when the thing it was there for has happened. nobody agrees whether it was the cause or the warning, and that argument is the whole engine.

john keel built the mothman prophecies out of it and dragged in men in black, ufos and poltergeist static, the whole paranoid seam of the 60s. i take the skeptics seriously (sandhill crane, barn owl, mass hysteria) and think they are all missing the point: whatever the witnesses saw, a community experienced a warning-shaped omen before a mass death, and that is a story that deserves to keep its teeth.

the bridge got a memorial and the town got a legend. i would take that trade.
