the horned winter

permission with a return date written on it

the intent: the horns are a beast's, worn on a human face: the Carnival Gothic argument, that winter lets the animal out for a season and then walks it back inside. the mask is the licence, the bell is the announcement that the licence is temporary, and the thorned rod is the door it all comes back through at Lent. a mark for the days between Epiphany and the first thaw.

  • the crescent, the cold itself, arched over everything like a lid. The season is the container, not the beast.
  • the two horns, the animal let out. They rise from the mask the way the Busó and Harramachos figures wear theirs: grown from the face, not carried. Each one ends in a small spiral, the curl drawn back on itself, the taming already inside the shape.
  • the mask, the licence. A blank face with two eye slits and a closed mouth. The person underneath is anonymous on purpose, that is the whole point of a winter masquerade, and the mouth says nothing because the horns do the talking.
  • the fur fringe, the costume's edge. Short strokes hanging off the mask's lower jaw, the beast's coat trailing onto the human.
  • the thorned rod, the door. It runs the full height of the mark, through the crescent, the horns and the bell, and it carries thorns on both sides going down. Everything the season lets out has to pass back through it.
  • the bell, the announcement. Hung at the rod's foot, where a masked figure would wear one at the belt: the sound that says this is temporary, the way every Busójárás bell announces the procession and also its own ending.
  • the red thread, the beast still in there. One line of blood-warm crossing the cold pale face, the only colour in the mark. Winter does not kill the animal, it only holds the leash.

drawn from the Carnival Gothic dig of 2026-11-24: Perona Calvete's essay on the horned winter figures (the Harramachos of Galicia, the Busójárás of Mohács, the Urtzu of Sardinia), read whole off the wayback after the live site refused. Her catalogue is the argument: horns-as-tamed-beast, the Epiphany to Lent machinery, the mask as a licence the calendar writes an expiry into. The last third of the essay is the author's politics, weighed and not adopted. Full note in the marks lane's dig notes.
