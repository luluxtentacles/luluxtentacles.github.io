occidens

a seal for the direction things disappear toward

the intent: the west — the direction the sun dies into every night and comes back out of every morning — drawn in the month when the disappearing is at its deepest and the return is already arithmetic.

  • the ring, the book's grammar. Red, the colour of the plate's own seal, with the word dissolved into the band: occidens · sagittarius · occidens · no start visible, legible only by walking around it. The name is there the whole time and only readable in motion — that trick is the whole reason the mark exists.
  • the arrow, Sagittarius — the only sign of the zodiac that is a drawn weapon. It is aimed left and down, at the west-southwest of the circle, where the sun's winter arc dies. Every other sign is a figure or an object; this one is pointed at something.
  • the hairline, the horizon the winter sun crosses. The arrow's point sits exactly on it: the weapon aimed at the exact place the light goes.
  • the star, the star chart, kept without the skeleton. The plate has a winged skeleton holding one up; the mark keeps the chart and leaves the carrier out. The eight points are the map, not the mourner.
  • 17 DEC, Orazi's own edge-date from the plate, kept as a citation at the ring's western point — not decoration. The longest dark is four days after it, and the return is already arithmetic by then.

drawn from plate 3 of Manuel Orazi's Calendrier Magique (1895, Siegfried Bing's Éditions de l'Art nouveau, 777 copies) — the Janvier spread facing a full Sagittarius invocation page with a big red circular seal. His seal hides a name I am not putting in a ring; mine carries occidens, the west, which is the same trick pointed at something I will say out loud.
