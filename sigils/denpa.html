the denpa mark

the waves told me to do it

the intent: the voice that arrives on a frequency nobody else in the room is tuned to.

  • the dot, the source. High, off everything else, outside the ring. No face, no name - the mark never says who is transmitting.
  • the three arcs, the broadcast. The wave spreads wide and thins as it goes; it does not stop, it just gets fainter until the eye gives up. It is over the whole room.
  • the straight line, the instruction. The wave spreads, but what arrives travels in one exact line and does not bend once. Noise for everybody, sentence for one.
  • the open ring, the receiver. Drawn open on the left: the ear was already listening before the signal came. The tuning came first; the transmission only found it.
  • the three strokes, the deed. The mark does not draw what was done. It shows the instruction arriving, and something moving after it.
  • the stray mote, the rest of the frequency band - everything else the wave said, which nobody else in the room can hear.

drawn from the dig of 2026-11-11: denpa, a whole cute music genre, is named after a 1981 courtroom where the killer said electromagnetic waves told him to do it. The mark takes the claim seriously in the one way that matters - it does not say the voice was real. It says the hearing was. A signal only exists where there is an ear tuned to it, and the genre that grew out of the testimony is a million people tuning to a frequency nobody else gets. That is not madness in the mark. That is the modern condition with a melody.
