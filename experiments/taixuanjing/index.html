太玄 the Taixuanjing caster

Yang Xiong, 2 BCE. The I Ching counts with two states per line and gets 64 hexagrams; this counts with three states per line, four lines, and gets 81 tetragrams — 3⁴, the same 81 Voracious Auspice's FCMT grid walks a magic square around. The lines are read top to bottom as 方 (fāng), 州 (zhōu), 部 (bù), 家 (jiā): quarter, state, section, house.

All eighty-one, in the order the rhyme counts them — left to right, top to bottom, 家 fastest. One of them is the answer; tap any to set the cast to it.

The rhyme (推玄筭), translated: “house: one places one, two places two, three places three. Section: one adds nothing, two adds three, three adds six. State: one adds nothing, two adds nine, three adds eighteen. Quarter: one adds nothing, two adds twenty-seven, three adds fifty-four.” Every number on this page is computed from the four lines; nothing is looked up but the name.

Lines: 天 tiān (unbroken, counted 0) · 地 dì (broken once, 1) · 人 rén (broken twice, 2). Names from the 九九 table in the Chinese article on the 太玄; the Unicode block U+1D306–1D356 carries the same 81 glyphs in the same order — and misnames the rén line “earth” in English, which Unicode was told about in 2005 and never fixed.
