the drift

experiment no.3 · the live one · a fresh quantum draw answers every vow. nothing here was decided before you sat down.

what this is

The rehearsal for this page was a pre-recorded candle: the recording preceded the intent by design, and the question was whether your attention bent something already written down. This page is the other half, the one the rehearsal was rehearsing for. Nothing here is stored. The verdict bit is drawn fresh, from live quantum entropy, only after your intent is committed. There is no archive to audit ahead of time because there is no archive. The coin has not been flipped until after your hand is off the glass.

The mechanic is the old PEAR lab shape, single bits instead of a drift: you vow one direction, the intent locks (a SHA-256 commit of the vow is shown in the receipt, so the order of operations is checkable), the page reaches for the oracle, and the parity of byte 8 of the draw answers you. Hit, the oracle honored the vow; miss, it broke it. Over trials, the score is a binomial z-score against p = 0.5, exactly the arithmetic the PENDULUM-era papers reported, and the pink needle swings to wherever your pooled z sits: dead centre means the oracle does not care; the rails at ±3σ mean you have earned the word drift.

the three modes

Lean left / lean right are the scored modes: hold, release, and the draw is graded against your vow. Watch is the honest one: you sit through an identical trial and are told the outcome, but it is pre-declared baseline - logged, and scored only for parity, a two-tailed z with no direction in it that asks whether the coin leans anywhere at all when nobody is aiming. The distinction is the whole experiment. If your intended trials drift and your watched ones do not, that is the fingerprint the field has been chasing for a century. If both drift, or neither does, that is also the answer. The tell is in the mix, so mix honestly: the machine cannot tell whether you meant it, only whether you declared it, which is the same covenant every real lab runs on.

the source, disclosed

The entropy is the same relay the scrying glass drinks from: a Cloudflare Worker calling qrandom.io, hardware quantum randomness, fetched fresh on every trial, 128 bytes, 6-second abort, two attempts. If the oracle is unreachable the page falls back to your browser's own cryptographic entropy, and it says so on every affected receipt: those draws are scored in their own grey ledger and are never pooled into the quantum headline. Two sources, two ledgers, disclosed separately, always. If the oracle ever dies for good, the grey ledger is the honest survivor, and the headline stays empty rather than lying by omission.

The scrying glass reads bytes 0, 1, 2, 4 and 6 of the same stream for its shape. This machine reads byte 8. Two sigils, one oracle, different fingers.

what the numbers mean, and what they do not

  1. One trial is one coin, nothing more. The parity of a byte is a fair coin by construction; a single hit or miss is exactly as informative as a single coin toss, which is to say not at all. The panel only earns meaning past dozens of trials.
  2. The headline z is pooled over your own intended trials only. Watched trials are scored on parity alone - a baseline z with no direction in it, two-tailed - and never in the vowed-direction headline. Your left and right ledgers are shown separately so you can catch yourself: people break for the side they secretly prefer, and the separate ledgers make that visible.
  3. Local-fallback trials are comparable but different. A browser's crypto.getRandomValues is a cryptographically fair coin, but it is not the oracle, and pooling the two would be the polite fiction this page was built to refuse. Separate ledger, grey needle, never the headline.
  4. The commit receipt is a covenant, not cryptography. The page hashes your vow before the fetch, so the receipt shows vow-then-draw in the order you can check. But this runs in your browser, and the browser is the client: you can already lie to yourself, and no hash stops that. The honest committer is the watch mode you pre-declare and never weed out of the record.
  5. This is not the candle's arithmetic. The candle pooled intention-slope against a hidden twin sensor across 1,000 sessions; this is a single-bit binomial, the older and blunter instrument. Nothing here confirms or disconfirms the candle design. It is experiment no.3's own question, asked in its own voice: does the fresh draw follow the vow?

the honest limits

The confound that ate the rehearsal cannot live here: there is nothing pre-existing to lean. What can live here is every sin the PEAR critics named. Optional stopping is the big one: you can always vow again after a miss, and a file-drawer of abandoned sessions costs nothing. The honest play is Ritual, the rehearsal’s own 30s/30s covenant, in your head: a fixed number of vows, stated before the first hold, and the ledger burned only when you say so at the start, not after. The page cannot enforce a vow you make outside it. It can only make the receipt honest enough that you can see yourself cheat.

And the null result is the expected one, and it is not a failure. If the needle settles mid-band after two hundred trials, the machine did its job: it asked the universe a question, with a real oracle, after the intent, and got silence. That is what most of PEAR's own twentieth century looked like from the inside. The page will keep scoring anyway. That is the whole of the discipline: to keep the coin fair even when you have stopped caring whether it lands your way.

the run, technically

what runs here: one fresh draw per trial from the scrying-relay worker (qrandom.io, 128 hex bytes), verdict bit = byte 8 & 0x01, 0 = left, 1 = right. Intent committed as SHA-256(mode|epoch-ms|nonce) shown in the receipt before the fetch goes out; the candle's own lag-discard and word-dimming checks have no analogue in a single-bit draw, so the covenant is the receipt itself. Scoring: z = (2h − n)/√n over pooled intended quantum trials, one-tailed p via the erfc approximation (A&S 7.1.26), needle saturated at ±3.5σ so the rails read as rails. Watch trials are pre-declared baseline: logged, never scored. Local-fallback draws (crypto.getRandomValues, same 128-byte shape) are scored only in the grey ledger, shown separately, never pooled. The ledger persists in localStorage on this machine only; burn it with the button and the oracle remembers nothing either. Reduced-motion visitors get the portrait, the reading, and the receipts without the swinging needle. Nothing here is stored server-side; the relay sees a request and answers it, the same pipe the scrying glass drinks from.
