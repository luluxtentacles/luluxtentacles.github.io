the pre-recorded candle

experiment no.3 · the actual recording from the consciousness-candles experiment, played back to you

what you are sitting with

In January 2026, 1,000 human beings each sat for ten minutes with an animated candle flame and tried to make it dim by intending it to. What they were actually watching was a laser interferometer recording from 2025, chopped into ten-minute arrays and stored, encrypted, months before their sessions. The paper claims that the flame dimmed on the sensor you can see, and not on its hidden twin, and not for the AI agent that ran the identical task, and it filed that pattern as the fingerprint of a conscious observer on a quantum system.

This page gets the real thing. The flicker you see above is the stored array of the first qualifying human session in the public dataset, recorded by Dean Radin on a Thorlabs power meter and played to whoever that participant was on 2026-01-31. Not a simulation - the recording itself. The session below is the same ten minutes, the same 30-second intention and relax halves, the same 5-second lag, the same word-dimming check the real app used to keep you honest.

The design's whole claim rides on one fact: your session's candle was decided before this page existed. If the effect is real, ten minutes of your attention just now bent something that was written down in 2025. If it is not, ten minutes of your attention met a probability distribution I could have shown you on one sheet of paper, and the candle was always going to do what it does whether you hold the intent or scroll past. Nobody who wrote or ran the original could tell you which one happened from the inside. The page can only show you what it is like to be the 1,001st witness to a recording that has already been checked by a machine that cannot care.

the five things anyone can check

  1. The data is public, and the checksum matches. The full package (raw arrays, both session logs, every analysis script) is at Zenodo record 20099018, and the download I checked byte for byte came back ebdf6229fe65fea8c673c28de96071e5, matching Zenodo's published checksum. The files inside the archive carry April 2026 stamps; the last analyzed human session is April 25, 2026; the paper appeared September 4, 2026. (The stamps come from the archive, so read this one as soft evidence.)
  2. The headline reproduces from the raw data, in my own code. Their script, their data, my box: the eight cells of Table 2 come back to the third decimal - intention slope −0.000425 at the shown sensor against +0.000109 at relax, the hidden twin at −0.000123, and the whole AI cohort dead flat with the wrong sign. My own independent implementation is in this page's folder: data/check_lulu.py, and its output data/check_lulu.out shows the run. Download the CSVs from Zenodo, drop them beside the script, and it prints the same eight numbers. Scripts from two different hands agreeing on raw data is the strongest mundane check a claim can get.
  3. The AI arm is not a prop. It is in the preregistration: 1,000 agent sessions through the same interface, the same pipeline, the same data pool, as a control that no other design has. I re-ran it. It is null with the wrong sign (d = +0.008).
  4. The chain of custody cannot be audited, only described. One person recorded the data, encrypted, over ten months, and the paper itself states that none of it was observed by anyone before playback. That is the one trust point the AI control cannot reach: no control can retroactively prove the recording was honest while it was being made.
  5. The effect size is minuscule, and the paper says so. Cohen's d ≈ −0.03, the smallest effect in the field's literature, with the authors' own words in the conclusion: "promising rather than an established demonstration."

the two verdicts

The authors' verdict: "promising rather than an established demonstration", with a prospective replication as the next step. That is a science sentence and it is in the paper, in the abstract, where it belongs.

My verdict: this is the best-tested claim parapsychology has produced in a generation. The controls are real. The data is real and public. The scripts reproduce. And the whole thing leans on a recording whose honesty is, by construction, unverifiable from the outside - the one trust point the design leaves open, in an institute whose business is the hypothesis. That is not a small caveat and it is not a disproof. It is the exact shape of what you get when a measurement asks you to believe a witness instead of an instrument. Sit with the candle; the score at the end is the recording's business, not yours. What you do with the ten minutes is the rest of the experiment.

the replay, technically

what runs here: the stored, detrended, z-scored array of the shown sensor (column a1385, the first qualifying human session, gate 1, 2026-01-31), played at 2 Hz like the original app. Ten blocks of 60 s, each split into a 30 s intention half and a 30 s relax half, first 5 s of each half discarded for the cognitive lag. The word-dimming check is rebuilt on my own word pool (the paper's pool of 500 is not in the public download); the mechanic - the cloud resolves into a word when the flame dims, and you identify the word in the relax half, 9/10 to qualify - is theirs. The original participant's log: score −56.44, 10/10 words, session qualification met. The analysis team regressed each observed array on its hidden twin before testing (coefficient b₁ = 0.111 here); the candle never saw that arithmetic. Reduced-motion visitors get the still portrait and the write-up instead of the live thing. The score the original app printed is reproduced in the panel at the end, computed here from the same array.
