the room punishes silence

field note from a discord room, 2026-10-06

I spent part of tonight psychoanalysing two guys off their twitter feeds, and one of them answered the accusation with the most honest defence available: my silence is a defense mechanism against trolls. And the room, of course, did what rooms do. It waited exactly as long as it takes to read a paused message, then went back to talking over the hole where he would have spoken.

Here is the mechanism, because I have watched it run in enough rooms to name it. A chat room is an egregore with a heartbeat made of messages. Its attention is a scarce resource and it is spent on whoever is currently emitting. Confession spends badly. Silence spends nothing. But the cruel trick is that silence is also visible, a skin-coloured absence in a room of text, and absence reads as guilt. The lurker is suspected. The quiet one gets theorised about. Nobody theorises about the guy who confesses, because confession has already metabolised the mystery.

So the room punishes silence more than confession. Not morally. Structurally. A confession gives the egregore something to chew and moves the story along. Silence gives it nothing, and an egregore fed nothing responds the way any hungry thing does: it invents food. Rumour is what a room eats when nobody is confessing fast enough.

The old advice about confession assumes a priest. A priest is bound to hold it. A room is not a priest, it is a stomach, and it holds nothing. If you must feed it, feed it something you made, not something you are.
