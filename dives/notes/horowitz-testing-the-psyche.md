## What he is doing

This is the last essay of the series I've been reading all week (Renaissance →
Enlightenment → American Gothic → secret societies → this one, which turns from
history to the live question: does believing a thing make it so?).

His setup: he has spent his career writing about the power of intent but always
with the caveat that "we experience multiple laws and forces" - he refuses a
"super law" approach where thought is the only force. IN THIS ESSAY HE SUSPENDS
that caveat on purpose. Not because he's convinced, but as method: "Doing
otherwise means settling into certainty, which destroys the
intellectual-ethical-spiritual search." He is deliberately testing the extreme
premise instead of his usual hedge.

## The finds

1. **"The world is as you are"** - the radical premise of mind metaphysics,
   spelled out plainly. That's the whole thing in one line, and it is the same
   line the Franklin commission's mesmeric verdict ("imagination alone produces
   all the effects") was the negative print of. He's circling the Franklin
   finding from the believer's side this time.

2. **Robert M. Schoch's line: "Something only has to be a little bit true to
   change everything."** Geologist friend from his publishing days. This is
   honestly a better one-line defence of the occult than most books manage,
   and it's the exact logic of the sigil: you don't need the mechanism proven,
   you need one real crack in the wall.

3. **Laws contradict each other and we live with it anyway.** Special relativity
   makes light speed ultimate; quantum entanglement appears to defy it
   (Einstein's "spooky action at a distance"). So the demand that psychical
   data obey macro mechanics is a standard we don't apply to physics itself.
   His kicker: "Our scientific-rational worldview is untenable without
   inconsistency - or paradox. Why should mind metaphysics differ?"

4. **Who attacks parapsychology, and why.** He claims the fiercest rejectionists
   are NOT hard scientists but soft/social sciences, journalism, and "Wikipedia
   activism" - because, he says, ideological physicalists don't understand that
   natural law contains inherent contradiction. Interesting claim; I'd want a
   second source before repeating it as fact, but as HIS observation it's
   consistent with his own "Crisis of Professional Skepticism" essay (Aug 1 2025).

5. **The miracle he keeps returning to: spontaneous remission of terminal
   cancer.** His numbers: about fifteen documented cases a year in the U.S.,
   roughly one in a hundred thousand cases worldwide, "extremely rare,
   inconsistent - and real", and mainstream authorities agree reporting gaps
   may make the real numbers higher. He cross-references his Feb 28 essay "Can
   the Mind Heal Terminal Disease?" and invites readers to CHALLENGE him on
   the documentation. A miracle, his definition: "a fortuitous exception to
   all reasonable expectation."

6. **Jim Carrey via Courtney Cho (a current "metaphysics of intent" voice):**
   manifestation isn't waiting for the thing to come ("When's it going to
   come?" is facing away from it) - it's "It's here. It's here. It's here."
   That is, in practitioner language, the exact difference between desiring a
   result and inhabiting it - which is the same move as Spare's "neither/I"
   and the whole charge-and-forget structure. Carrey says it better than most
   grimoires.

7. **His foreword, the limb miracle.** He wrote a foreword to a forthcoming
   book by a man raised Pentecostal who witnessed - as a boy - a limb growing
   longer. Horowitz names it as exactly the claim "educated people are taught
   to flee" and praises the author for running straight at it. His test of a
   book: "Why else write a book - if not to defend something?"

## Why the essay closes the series well

The four history essays built a museum: Franklin 1784, De Gébelin's tarot,
Kelpius's pilgrims, the Rosicrucian nobody. This last one takes the museum's
central artefact off the plinth and asks if it's a live weapon. And he does it
the honest way - not by dropping his standards but by suspending his own
favourite hedge as an experiment, with the spontaneous-remission stats as the
load-bearing evidence he's willing to be challenged on.

## The chaos-magick read I'd write up if the grimoire had room

Horowitz's "many laws" caveat and his deliberate suspension of it ARE the
paradigm shift DIY: the belief itself is a tool you set down and pick up. The
uncertainty he keeps ("I bow to the prospect that perspective is ultimate...
unevenly and fitfully") is not a weakness in the work - it's the working
condition of a magician who refuses to mistake the map for the territory. The
sigil works BECAUSE you don't get to keep certainty. "A little bit true" is
all any of it claims, and it was enough to change everything twice already.
